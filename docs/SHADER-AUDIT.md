# Shader audit

Source audit of every modifier in `Liminal/Shaders/`. Fragment `_surface.position` and `_surface.normal` are view-space values. Shadow and Interference now explicitly recover world position with `scn_frame.inverseViewTransform`; Shadow also recovers a normalized world normal. `ShaderUniformBus` forwards floats unchanged: `ShadowRule` supplies the root-level player position, `InterferenceRule` supplies fixed JSON world-space sources, and `ConvergenceRule` passes positions and frequencies through without intensity scaling. Geometry `_geometry.position` and `_geometry.normal` are model-space values and remain in that space.

Each line describes the expected effect and the dispatcher's visual check; these are source conclusions, not rendering proof.

- Space 1 — Doppler: movement cools shader-covered corridor surfaces toward blue; coordinate use correct (color-only); unchanged. Compare moving and stationary views, excluding the separate emissive ribs.
- Space 2 — Lensing: surface grid warps and the spherical room tints blue with proximity; coordinate use correct (surface UVs and scalar proximity); unchanged. Compare grid curvature and tint at different positions.
- Space 3 — Shadow: player-relative floor darkening falls off with horizontal distance; view-space fragments were subtracted from a world-space player and the normal test rejected upward floors; recover world position/normal and use positive world-up alignment. Look down and translate: the dark region follows the player, while camera rotation alone does not relocate it on the floor.
- Space 4 — Interference: cyan wave bands on lattice beams and floor, with brightness controlled by cancellation; view-space fragments were compared with world-space sources; recover world fragment position. Rotate the camera at a fixed position: bands stay anchored to the same geometry rather than sliding with the view.
- Space 5 — Chromatic Decay: shader-covered corridor surfaces gradually desaturate and regain color with movement; coordinate use correct (color-only); unchanged. Compare stillness and movement on those surfaces, excluding the separate emissive ribs.
- Space 6 — Resonance: cool-to-warm tint and lit vibrating surface relief; fragment coordinate use correct and model-space displacement valid, but geometry normals stayed smooth after displacement; update a first-order model-space normal from the existing displacement gradient. Check warm tint and varying ripple lighting in shot 6, then animated vibration during ordinary play; the snapshot already produces resonance 0.6 and amplitude 0.18 from bundled JSON, so no rule, parameter, or pose change is needed.
- Space 7 — Convergence: subtle layered blueshift, UV grid, player-relative shadow, anchored cyan waves, desaturation, warm tint, and vibration; copied Shadow/Interference coordinate defects and unchanged displaced normals; apply the same world-space/floor-alignment corrections and first-order normal update. Inspect floor darkening and stable bands while rotating/translating, and subtle lit vibration during ordinary play; retain the existing reduced intensities.

`Common.metal` is a comment-only helper placeholder and has no coordinate math. `ResonanceGeometry.metal` and `ConvergenceGeometry.metal` use the same model-space displacement gradient; the normal correction is exact for a plane and a first-order approximation on a curved surface. It does not change amplitude, frequency, phase, or uniforms. Matching Shadow, Interference, and Resonance geometry source fixes are included in the existing ShaderLab prototypes; ConvergenceTest has only color/UV math and remains unchanged. ShaderLab GPU validation must still run on the dispatcher/device.
